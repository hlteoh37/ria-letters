Risk Tolerance Letter for Financial Advisors: Templates & Best Practices

Understanding a client's risk tolerance is one of the most critical steps in building a sound investment plan. Yet many advisors treat risk assessment as a one-time checkbox exercise during onboarding. Sending a thoughtful risk tolerance letter — whether to initiate a new assessment, share results, or revisit tolerance after a major life event — strengthens the advisory relationship and creates essential compliance documentation.

Why Risk Tolerance Letters Matter

Regulators expect advisors to demonstrate that investment recommendations are suitable for each client. A well-crafted risk tolerance letter serves as documented evidence of your suitability process. It shows you actively assessed the client's capacity and willingness to take risk before making portfolio decisions.

Beyond compliance, these letters build trust. When clients understand how their risk profile maps to their portfolio allocation, they are less likely to panic during market downturns. Proactive communication about risk reduces emotional decision-making — the single biggest threat to long-term investment returns.

Risk tolerance should be reassessed periodically and after significant life changes: retirement, inheritance, divorce, health events, or major market moves. Each reassessment deserves a clear, professional letter explaining the process and results.

Key Elements of a Risk Tolerance Letter

  • Purpose statement — Explain why you are conducting or revisiting the risk assessment
  • Assessment methodology — Describe the questionnaire or process you use
  • Risk profile summary — Translate scores into plain English with clear definitions
  • Portfolio alignment — Show how their allocation maps to their assessed risk level
  • Next steps — Outline what happens next: review meeting, adjustments, or confirmation
  • Client acknowledgment — Include a section for the client to confirm their risk profile

Sample Risk Tolerance Assessment Letter

Dear [Client Name],

As part of our commitment to ensuring your investment strategy remains aligned with your financial goals and comfort level, we are conducting our [annual/periodic] risk tolerance review. Understanding your current risk preferences is essential to making sound investment decisions on your behalf.

Enclosed you will find our Risk Tolerance Questionnaire, which covers key areas including your investment time horizon, income stability, prior investment experience, and emotional comfort with market fluctuations. The questionnaire takes approximately 10-15 minutes to complete.

Based on your responses, we will categorize your risk profile along our five-point scale: Conservative, Moderately Conservative, Moderate, Moderately Aggressive, or Aggressive. We will then review your current portfolio allocation of [current allocation summary] to ensure it remains appropriate for your assessed risk level.

If your risk tolerance has changed since our last assessment in [previous assessment date], we will schedule a review meeting to discuss potential portfolio adjustments. Any changes will be made only with your explicit approval.

Please complete and return the enclosed questionnaire by [deadline]. If you prefer, we can complete the assessment together during a phone call or video meeting.

Sincerely,
[Advisor Name], [Credentials]
[Firm Name]

Best Practices and Compliance Tips

  1. Use a standardized assessment tool — Consistency in methodology strengthens your compliance documentation and makes it easier to compare results over time
  2. Document the conversation, not just the score — Regulators want to see that you discussed risk with the client in terms they understood
  3. Reassess after life events — Marriage, divorce, retirement, job loss, inheritance, or health changes can dramatically shift risk tolerance
  4. Distinguish capacity from willingness — A client may have a long time horizon but lose sleep over a 10% drawdown. Address both dimensions
  5. Keep historical records — Maintain copies of all risk assessments. If a client later claims their portfolio was too aggressive, these letters are your primary defense

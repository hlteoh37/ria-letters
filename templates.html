Owner Letter Templates

Professional monthly narrative letters you can copy, customize, and send to property owners. Built for property managers who want professional owner communication without starting from a blank page.

Why these templates exist

Most property management software (Buildium, AppFolio, Rent Manager) generates financial statements. What owners actually want is a narrative letter — a plain-English summary of what happened with their property this month, written by someone who manages it.

These templates cover the most common monthly reporting scenarios:

  • Stable occupied property — no issues, normal month
  • Maintenance repair completed — explaining work done and costs
  • Vacancy — property turned over, back on market
  • Rent delinquency — handling late payment professionally
  • Lease renewal — proactive communication with good news

Usage: These are starting points. Replace bracketed fields with real data. For 20+ properties, see the automated option at the bottom of this page.

Stable Occupied Property — Normal Month

Most common scenario
Use when: Nothing notable happened, rent collected, no issues
Tone: Professional, reassuring
Subject: Monthly Property Report — [PROPERTY ADDRESS] — [MONTH YEAR] Dear [OWNER FIRST NAME], I'm writing with your monthly update for [PROPERTY ADDRESS]. [MONTH] was a quiet and productive month for your property. Your tenant, [TENANT NAME], paid rent of $[AMOUNT] on time on [DATE]. There are no outstanding maintenance issues, no lease concerns, and no delinquencies to report. Current occupancy status: Occupied Monthly rent collected: $[AMOUNT] Outstanding balance: $0.00 Active maintenance requests: None The property continues to perform well. I'll reach out immediately if anything requiring your attention comes up before next month's report. Please don't hesitate to contact me with any questions. Best regards, [YOUR NAME] [COMPANY NAME] [PHONE] | [EMAIL]

Customization tips: Add a line about the local rental market if you have relevant data (e.g., "Comparable units in the area are renting for $X, putting your property slightly above/below market"). Owners appreciate context, not just status.

2

Maintenance Work Completed

Explain work + cost
Use when: Repair or maintenance was performed this month
Tone: Transparent, proactive
Subject: Monthly Property Report — [PROPERTY ADDRESS] — [MONTH YEAR] Dear [OWNER FIRST NAME], Here is your monthly update for [PROPERTY ADDRESS]. FINANCIAL SUMMARY Rent collected: $[AMOUNT] (received [DATE]) Maintenance costs this month: $[MAINTENANCE TOTAL] Net to owner: $[NET AMOUNT] MAINTENANCE COMPLETED This month we addressed [BRIEF DESCRIPTION — e.g., "a plumbing issue reported by the tenant on [DATE]"]. [VENDOR NAME] completed the work on [COMPLETION DATE]. Work performed: [DESCRIPTION OF WORK] Vendor: [VENDOR NAME] Invoice amount: $[COST] Reason: [ONE SENTENCE EXPLANATION — e.g., "The water heater's pressure relief valve failed and required replacement to prevent potential water damage."] This is a [routine / preventive / emergency] repair. [Optional: "We sourced two quotes before selecting [VENDOR NAME] — the other quote was $[COMPETING QUOTE]."] CURRENT STATUS Occupancy: Occupied Lease expires: [DATE] Open maintenance requests: [NUMBER] I've attached the invoice for your records. Please let me know if you have any questions about the work performed. Best regards, [YOUR NAME] [COMPANY NAME] [PHONE] | [EMAIL]

Customization tips: Always explain why work was done, not just what. Owners who understand the reason for a repair are far less likely to dispute costs. If you got multiple quotes, mention it — it builds enormous trust.

3

Vacancy — Tenant Moved Out

Turn and re-lease
Use when: Tenant vacated, property being prepared for re-listing
Tone: Action-oriented, confident
Subject: Vacancy Update — [PROPERTY ADDRESS] — [MONTH YEAR] Dear [OWNER FIRST NAME], I'm writing with an important update on [PROPERTY ADDRESS]. Your previous tenant, [TENANT NAME], vacated the property on [MOVE-OUT DATE] at the end of their lease. The move-out inspection was completed on [INSPECTION DATE]. MOVE-OUT SUMMARY Security deposit held: $[AMOUNT] Security deposit disposition: [Return in full / Partial deduction of $X for [reason]] Property condition: [Brief assessment — e.g., "Generally good condition. Minor wall scuffs and carpet wear consistent with normal occupancy."] TURN STATUS Make-ready work required: [List — e.g., "Paint touch-ups, professional carpet cleaning, HVAC filter replacement"] Estimated make-ready cost: $[AMOUNT] Expected ready-to-show date: [DATE] RE-LEASING PLAN Listing price: $[AMOUNT]/month (comparable units are renting for $[RANGE]) Marketing: [Zillow, Apartments.com, MLS, local signage] Projected lease start: [DATE RANGE] I've already [begun / scheduled] the make-ready work and will send updated photos once the property is show-ready. Inquiries are [already coming in / expected once listing goes live]. I'll keep you updated weekly until the vacancy is filled. My target is to minimize days vacant while securing a qualified tenant. Best regards, [YOUR NAME] [COMPANY NAME] [PHONE] | [EMAIL]

Customization tips: Vacancy letters are high-stakes — owners get anxious about empty units and lost income. Lead with your action plan. The faster you communicate "here's what I'm doing about it," the more confidence you build. Include a photo of the property if possible.

Rent Delinquency

Late or missed payment
Use when: Tenant is late or hasn't paid rent
Tone: Factual, professional, calm
Subject: Rent Delinquency Notice — [PROPERTY ADDRESS] — [MONTH YEAR] Dear [OWNER FIRST NAME], I'm writing to inform you of a rent delinquency at [PROPERTY ADDRESS] and to update you on the steps we're taking. DELINQUENCY SUMMARY Tenant: [TENANT NAME] Rent due date: [DATE] Amount due: $[AMOUNT] Current status: [e.g., "Payment not received as of [DATE]" / "Partial payment of $X received"] Late fee assessed: $[AMOUNT] (per lease terms) ACTIONS TAKEN [DATE]: Sent 3-day pay or quit notice via [certified mail / email / posted to door] [DATE]: Attempted phone contact — [reached tenant / no answer / left voicemail] [DATE]: [Any additional follow-up] TENANT'S RESPONSE [e.g., "Tenant contacted our office on [DATE] and stated [brief explanation — e.g., 'they experienced a temporary income disruption and expect to pay in full by [DATE].'"] OR "We have not received a response from the tenant as of this writing."] NEXT STEPS If payment is not received by [DATE], we will [proceed with eviction filing / continue collection efforts]. I will keep you informed of any developments. [Optional: "Based on this tenant's [X-year] history with no prior issues, I recommend [X]. However, this is entirely your decision and I will follow your direction."] Best regards, [YOUR NAME] [COMPANY NAME] [PHONE] | [EMAIL]

Customization tips: Delinquency letters require factual precision — dates, amounts, actions taken. Owners need to see that you're on top of it. Always include what you've already done, not just what's happening. If you have a recommendation, state it clearly but make clear it's the owner's call.

Lease Renewal Secured

Good news — tenant staying
Use when: Tenant signed renewal or agreed to stay
Tone: Positive, value-demonstrating
Subject: Lease Renewal Confirmed — [PROPERTY ADDRESS] — [MONTH YEAR] Dear [OWNER FIRST NAME], Great news to report for [PROPERTY ADDRESS] this month. LEASE RENEWAL CONFIRMED Tenant: [TENANT NAME] New lease term: [START DATE] through [END DATE] ([X] months) New monthly rent: $[NEW AMOUNT] (increased from $[OLD AMOUNT] — a [X]% increase) Lease signed: [DATE] This renewal means zero vacancy, zero turn costs, and continued stable income for your property. At current market rates of $[MARKET RANGE]/month for comparable units, your new rent of $[AMOUNT] positions the property [competitively / slightly below market to retain a reliable, long-term tenant]. TENANT NOTES [TENANT NAME] has been in residence since [MOVE-IN DATE] — [X] year(s). Payment history: [e.g., "consistent on-time payments throughout tenancy"]. No outstanding maintenance issues. FINANCIAL IMPACT Avoided vacancy cost: ~$[ESTIMATE] (based on [X] weeks average vacancy + turn costs in this market) Rent increase: +$[MONTHLY INCREASE]/month = +$[ANNUAL INCREASE]/year I'll send the executed lease copy to your file. Please let me know if you have any questions. Best regards, [YOUR NAME] [COMPANY NAME] [PHONE] | [EMAIL]

Customization tips: This is your opportunity to show value. Calculate and explicitly state the avoided vacancy cost — most owners don't think about it. A $1,200 rent increase sounds good; "this renewal avoided ~$3,200 in turnover costs" sounds like great property management.

Common questions about owner letters

How often should property managers send owner letters?

Monthly is the industry standard for active rentals. Some PMs send quarterly for stable, long-term tenancies with no issues. The key is consistency — owners who don't hear from their PM regularly start to wonder what's happening with their asset.

What's the difference between an owner letter and a financial statement?

Financial statements (income/expense reports) show numbers. Owner letters provide narrative context — explaining why costs occurred, what's happening with the tenancy, and what to expect next month. Most PM software generates statements automatically; the narrative letter requires judgment and writing, which is why it's time-consuming to do at scale.

Should I send owner letters via email or mail?

Email is standard for most property owners under 65. For older owners, or those who explicitly prefer it, physical mail builds trust. Many PMs use owner portals (via Buildium, AppFolio) to deliver the letter alongside the financial statement in a single communication.

What should I include in every monthly owner letter?

At minimum: rent collected and date received, any maintenance activity and costs, current occupancy status, and any upcoming lease events (renewals, inspections). Optional but valuable: brief market context, and a forward-looking note on what to expect next month.

How long should an owner letter be?

Short enough to read in 2 minutes. Most owners scan, not read. Use clear sections, bolded headers, and numbered lists for financial data. Reserve prose for context and explanations that numbers can't convey.

Client Birthday Letter for Financial Advisors: Templates & Ideas

The client birthday letter is one of the most underutilized relationship-building tools in a financial advisor's communication calendar. Done well, it demonstrates that you see clients as people, not accounts. Done poorly — or not at all — it represents a missed touchpoint in a relationship where every positive interaction compounds over time. With 100+ client relationships to manage, scaling meaningful birthday communications requires the right template and the right tools.

More Than "Happy Birthday": The Value of a Substantive Birthday Letter

Most advisors who send birthday communications send a generic card or a brief email that reads exactly like something sent by a database. Clients recognize this immediately. The opportunity is to write something that acknowledges the relationship specifically — a recent conversation, a shared milestone, or a relevant planning note tied to the birthday itself.

For clients approaching milestone birthdays (50, 59½, 62, 65, 70½, 73), the birthday letter is an exceptional opportunity to introduce age-specific financial planning topics in a warm, personal context. A client turning 59½ genuinely benefits from a birthday letter that mentions penalty-free IRA withdrawal access; a client turning 65 needs to know about Medicare enrollment windows.

Milestone Birthday Letters by Age

  • 50 — catch-up contribution eligibility reminder (IRA and 401k)
  • 55 — rule of 55 for 401k withdrawals if separated from employer
  • 59½ — penalty-free IRA and retirement account access begins
  • 62 — earliest Social Security eligibility (with permanent reduction)
  • 65 — Medicare enrollment window opens (Initial Enrollment Period)
  • 67 — full retirement age for most clients (born 1960 or later)
  • 70 — maximum Social Security delayed credits reached
  • 73 — RMDs begin under current law

Sample Client Birthday Letter (Milestone Age)

Dear [Client Name],

Happy [milestone age]th birthday! I hope you're celebrating this milestone in a way that reflects the great year you've had.

I always like to use milestone birthdays as a moment to flag something financially relevant — and turning [age] actually comes with some meaningful changes to your planning options.

[Relevant milestone note — e.g., "Starting this year, you're eligible for catch-up contributions to your IRA and 401(k). This means you can contribute an additional $1,000/year to your IRA and an additional $7,500 to your 401(k) — a meaningful boost to your savings in your final working years."]

If you'd like to talk through how to take advantage of this, it would be a great agenda item for our next meeting. Regardless, I hope today is a wonderful celebration with the people you love.

Warm wishes,
[Advisor Name]

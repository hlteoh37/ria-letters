Form CRS Delivery Letter for RIAs: Cover Letter Templates & Guide (2026)

Form CRS (Customer Relationship Summary) is a two-page disclosure document that registered investment advisors and broker-dealers must provide to retail investors. While the form itself is standardized, the cover letter you send with it is not — and it's an important opportunity to make the disclosure feel personal and useful rather than like regulatory paperwork.

What Is Form CRS?

Adopted by the SEC in 2019 as part of Regulation Best Interest, Form CRS describes in plain English:

  • What types of services you offer
  • The fees, costs, conflicts of interest, and standards of conduct associated with your services
  • Whether you have any legal or disciplinary history
  • How to get more information (including the BrokerCheck/IAPD links)

The form uses standardized headers and conversation starters like "Given my financial situation, should I choose an investment advisory service? Why or why not?" These are designed to help clients ask better questions — your cover letter can help prime them to actually do so.

When Must You Deliver Form CRS?

  • Before or at the time you enter into an investment advisory agreement with a new retail client
  • Before or at the time of a recommendation, if you're a dual registrant
  • Within 30 days if a client requests it
  • Within 60 days of filing an amended CRS with material changes — you must deliver the updated form to existing retail clients

Compliance Note: Form CRS delivery must be documented. Keep records showing when, how, and to whom you delivered the form. SEC examiners have cited firms for failing to document CRS delivery. Use email with read receipts, a client portal with delivery tracking, or obtain a written acknowledgment. The form itself must not be modified — you can add a cover letter, but the Form CRS content must remain as filed.

Sample Form CRS Delivery Cover Letter

[Date]


[Client Full Name]
[Client Address]
[City, State, ZIP]


Dear [Client First Name],


Thank you for choosing [Firm Name] as your investment advisor. As required by SEC regulations, I'm providing you with our Form CRS (Customer Relationship Summary), enclosed with this letter.


Form CRS is a short document — just two pages — that summarizes who we are, what services we provide, how we're compensated, and our legal obligations to you. I encourage you to read it before our first meeting.


A few things I'd like to highlight:

  • We are a fiduciary. As a Registered Investment Advisor, we are legally required to act in your best interest at all times — not just when it's convenient.
  • How we're compensated. We charge an annual advisory fee of [e.g., 1.00% on assets under management]. We do not receive commissions or third-party compensation for recommending specific investments.
  • Your questions matter. Form CRS includes suggested conversation starters at the bottom of each page. I welcome any and all questions about our services, fees, or how we're different from other advisors you've considered.

You can find our most current Form CRS on file with the SEC at www.adviserinfo.sec.gov (search for "[Firm Name]" or our CRD number [XXXXXXX]).


Please review the enclosed Form CRS at your convenience. If you have any questions, I'm happy to discuss it before, during, or after our initial meeting.


Warm regards,


[Advisor Name]
[Title]
[Firm Name]
[Phone] · [Email]
CRD #[XXXXXXX]

Updated CRS: Material Change Delivery Letter

When you file an amended Form CRS with material changes, you must deliver the updated form to all existing retail clients within 60 days. The delivery letter for material changes should briefly describe what changed and why, so clients don't need to compare the old and new versions line by line. Be specific: "We updated our fee schedule in Item 3 to reflect our new planning retainer option" is far more useful than "We updated our Form CRS."
